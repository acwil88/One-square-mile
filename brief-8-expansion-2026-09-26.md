## 2. Charles J. Canda

**Status: identity verified; the specific Section 7 conveyance still needs clerk-portal
confirmation.** Canda is no longer a mystery figure — he was a prominent New York financier.

- **Charles J. Canda (1838–1914).** Dates come from the University of Pennsylvania's Online
  Books Page library authority heading:
  https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?type=lcsubc&key=Canda%2C+Charles+J.%2C+1838-1914+--+Finance&c=x
  (This corrects the earlier working guess of ~1909 for his death year.)
- **Texas Pacific Land Trust trustee.** The official TPLT declaration of trust, dated
  February 1, 1888, names **Charles J. Canda, Simeon J. Drake, and William Strauss as
  trustees**, and states the T&P Railway and its mortgage trustees/receivers conveyed lands
  to them through **39 deeds dated August 4, 1887, expressly including Midland County.**
  Canda acknowledged the declaration in New York in May 1888. Source (SEC):
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/97517/000093244003000112/texaspacificdecl_oftrust.txt
- **Proprietor of former T&P lands.** A contemporaneous 1890 Glasscock County map
  description identifies Canda, Drake, and Strauss as proprietors of lands "heretofore
  belonging to the Texas and Pacific Railway Company," with W. H. Abrams as general agent
  in Dallas — i.e., the trust was actively selling its Texas acreage:
  https://relicmaps.com/products/map-of-glasscock-county-texas-pennsylvania-united-states-1890-poster-print-gift-18093
- **Litigating Texas land title.** An 1895 federal title case identifies Canda, Drake, and
  Strauss as New York citizens party to litigation over Texas land:
  https://static.case.law/f/67/case-pdfs/0001-01.pdf
- **Business interests.** A later New Jersey estate case identifies Canda's interests in
  **Canda Bros., the Chrome Steel Works, and the Canda Realty Company**, plus sons Charles
  A. and Abeel and three daughters. It does not establish a death date:
  https://static.case.law/nj-eq/92/case-pdfs/0423-01.pdf
- **Iron/steel correspondence.** The Social Networks and Archival Context (SNAC) record for
  Canda shows correspondence with the Cleveland-Cliffs Iron Company, consistent with his
  Chrome Steel Works interest:
  https://snaccooperative.org/view/74600102
- NYPL holds a portrait entry for Charles J. Canda:
  https://digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/510d47de-4e57-a3d9-e040-e00a18064a99
- Secondary genealogy material describes him as an Assistant U.S. Treasurer in New York,
  and secondary political references call him Democratic National Committee treasurer
  1875–1892. **Both need primary-source confirmation before printing.**
- **(Inference)** The Canda in the Midland deed chain is the New York financier/trustee:
  the trust received T&P's Midland County lands in 1887–88, began selling them through a
  Dallas agent, and source-log row 30 records Mary T. Edwards acquiring Section 7 from
  Canda directly. The specific deed must still be tied to the clerk record before this is
  stated as proven.

**Map margin candidate:** "Charles J. Canda (1838–1914), NY financier & Texas Pacific Land
Trust trustee — sold former T&P lands to Mary T. Edwards (deed TBD)."

## 3. HAILCO INC.

**Status: identified as a Midland-area homebuilder active by 1980; principals and exact
role in the 1981–82 Green Tree transactions still unconfirmed.**

- A Texas appellate opinion proves Hailco was a Midland residential builder by 1980. In
  **Macha v. Carameros, 674 S.W.2d 491 (Tex. App.—El Paso 1984)**, Alex Carameros redeemed
  ten Midland County city lots in April 1980 and sold five of them to **Hailco, Inc. and
  Personality Homes, Inc.; the companies constructed homes on those lots.** Both companies
  were represented by Midland counsel John G. Hyde. Source:
  https://static.case.law/sw2d/674/case-pdfs/0491-01.pdf
- This disproves the earlier impression that Hailco was wholly unidentified. It does **not**
  prove Hailco's principals or its exact role in the Green Tree plats.
- **(Inference)** The Hailco on the 1981–82 Green Tree plats is the same Hailco, Inc. from
  the 1984 case: same county, same two-year window, same line of business (residential
  construction). Strong but still circumstantial — shared officers or a corporate filing
  would close it.
- Do not confuse with Hailco USA Corporation, formed in 1998 — a different entity.

**Map margin candidate:** "HAILCO INC. — Midland homebuilder, building on city lots by
1980 (Macha v. Carameros, 1984); principals TBD."

## 4. Charles Howard

**Status: professional identity confirmed as an Austin-based golf-course architect;
personal biography still thin.**

- The official Champion Lakes course page names the architect as **"Charles Howard, P.E.
  of Austin, Texas"**:
  https://championlakesgolf.com/golf-course/course-details
- Green Tree's own material credits Charles Howard of Austin with the club's original
  18-hole course:
  https://www.texascmaa.org/files/Green%20tree%20CC%20Executive%20Chef%20Posting%20-%202025.pdf
- Visit Amarillo credits Howard with the original **Tomahawk Course at Comanche Trail Golf
  Complex**, opened July 1990:
  https://www.visitamarillo.com/listing/comanche-trail-golf-complex/520/
- Blackhawk Golf Club (Pflugerville), opened circa 1990–91, is credited to Charles Howard
  working with three-time U.S. Women's Open champion Hollis Stacy:
  https://www.city-data.com/guides/tx/austin/golf-blackhawk-golf-club-6144.html
  https://www.allsquaregolf.com/golf-courses/texas/blackhawk-golf-club
- That gives a documented portfolio of at least four Texas courses (Green Tree ~1980,
  Comanche Trail 1990, Blackhawk ~1990, Champion Lakes), spanning a decade of practice.
- Full middle name, firm name, dates, obituary, and professional biography remain
  unconfirmed. Safe printed description: **Charles Howard, P.E., Austin-based golf-course
  architect.**

**Map margin candidate:** "Course architect: Charles Howard, P.E. of Austin — Green Tree
(1980), Tomahawk/Comanche Trail (1990), Blackhawk, Champion Lakes."

## 6. Immediate-area pre-railroad history

**Status: regional context verified; nothing named or specific to Section 7 found.**

- **No named Indigenous trail, campsite, spring, or water source has been verified on this
  exact section.** Generic Comanche framing should not be placed on the sheet.
- Regional context is solid: the Comanches dominated West Texas from about 1700, displacing
  the Apaches, and used trails between water sources (Odessa-area history):
  http://www.odessahistory.com/preface.htm
- The documented **Comanche War Trail** ran from Big Spring (a known Comanche watering
  stop) south past Horsehead Crossing to Comanche Springs at Fort Stockton. Big Spring is
  roughly 35–40 miles northeast of Section 7, and the trail's documented route does not
  cross this section — it cannot be claimed for the mile:
  https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=201374
- The one watercourse that *does* cross the section is **Midland Draw**: the Handbook of
  Texas notes that "Midland Draw runs through the northern section of the city limits,"
  and Powell's 1876 field notes place the survey in the "waters of North Concho":
  https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/midland-tx
- Railroad-era anchor (for contrast, already established): the T&P established Midway
  Station in June 1881; first permanent resident Herman N. Garrett arrived 1882 with a
  herd of sheep; post office granted January 4, 1884; an Ohio real estate firm bought land
  and formed the Midland Town Company in early 1884; Midland County organized March 1885
  (Handbook of Texas, same source).
- **(Inference)** Midland Draw, a dry streambed, is exactly the kind of watercourse that
  anchored travel and grazing in the horse era — but no specific pre-1881 use of this
  section is documented, and the sheet should say so.

**Map margin candidate:** "Pre-1881: no named trail, camp, or spring documented on this
section; Comanche War Trail passed ~35 mi NE via Big Spring."
